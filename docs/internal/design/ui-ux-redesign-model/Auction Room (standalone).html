The Auction Room · as-built frames

The last five minutes, the one mechanic, and what everyone learns after the close.

These four pieces were owner-directed and built straight into the house style during the auction-operations pass (plan_design Pass 9e) — the drawings did not exist first, so these frames are the record, drawn from the shipped markup. Proxy bidding is the site’s only auction mechanic; the soft close is effectively unbounded (each reset demands new money, so a war converges on its own); everything below is live at listing_detail.html, my_bids.html and hunt.html.

Why a feed, not a number

Most bids land in the last minute, and a lone price that silently changes reads as a broken page. Five minutes out the room opens: every bid lands as a row (newest first, five at most), proxy answers say · auto, the price pulses when it moves, and the countdown breathes rust under two minutes. Reduced motion stills all of it.

The extension announces itself

A bid inside the final two minutes resets the clock to two minutes — losing volleys included, because the answer deserves the same window. Said three ways: the bidder’s toast (“The clock reset — two more minutes”), the room’s note, and the clock line’s “extended ×2”. Effectively unbounded; a 100-reset backstop guards pathology only.

When it closes under your eyes

The poll that notices the clock ran out is the same request that closes the lot server-side (the lazy close). The page then swaps to the outcome — the won-panel, the sold line — instead of freezing on a dead countdown. “EXPIRED” is retired; the beat between end and truth reads “Closing…”.

A2

How bidding works here

the ? beside the bid label, open
We bid for you.

Enter the most you’d pay. The price only climbs when somebody pushes it — your standing maximum answers one increment at a time, up to your number and never past it. Nobody ever sees your maximum.

If two maximums tie, the earlier one keeps the lead. You pay the price on the board at the close, not your maximum. Already ahead? Entering a higher number just raises your ceiling.

Bids are binding, and a bid in the closing minutes gives everyone a little more time.

One mechanic, said once

Proxy is the site’s only auction type (owner’s call — the eBay model, per the charter), so there is no per-lot badge taxonomy to learn. This popout is the whole education, one tap from the box it explains. The losing-volley toast is a warning, not a celebration: “The high bidder’s maximum covers $X — raise your maximum to take it.”

A6

For the build

where it all lives — this doc records shipped code
One payload for the room

bid_status carries: price, count, minimum, end, server_time (client clocks corrected), extensions, reserve_met, the last-8 feed (amount, bidder, auto, at), per-viewer is_leading/your_max, and outcome once closed. The poll also runs the lazy close.

Cadence

10s at a distance · 5s inside ten minutes · 2s inside the closing window · 30s when the tab is hidden · instant re-poll on tab return. Plain polling on purpose — server-rendered Django on a t3.micro; no sockets at this scale.

The constants

SOFT_CLOSE_WINDOW = 2 min, SOFT_CLOSE_CAP = 100 (backstop, not policy) — both in apps/bids/services.py, beside place_bid (proxy resolution) and close_auction (the one close, lazy everywhere, cron-swept).

Deliberately out

Second-chance offers to the runner-up (10.9’s note stands) · per-lot auction types (one mechanic, one education) · a permanent closed-lot browse (13.6’s business).
